Filing for divorce in Illinois doesn't have to mean hiring an expensive attorney. If you and your spouse agree on the major issues — property, debts, and children — you can often handle the process yourself. Here's what you need to know.

## Step 1: Meet the Residency Requirement

Illinois law includes a 90-day residence condition tied to starting the case or entry of judgment, depending on timing. Verify how residence, venue, and any military-service facts apply before filing.

## Step 2: Grounds for Divorce

Illinois is a no-fault divorce state. You only need to state that "irreconcilable differences" have caused the breakdown of the marriage. You don't need to prove fault or blame.

## Step 3: Gather Your Documents

Before you file, collect:
- Marriage certificate
- Financial records (bank accounts, debts, property)
- If children are involved: information about custody and support

## Step 4: Complete the Required Forms

Illinois requires several forms to start the divorce process:
- **Petition for Dissolution of Marriage** — the main filing document
- **Summons** — notifies your spouse of the filing
- **CCDR forms** — used in Cook County and other circuits

The specific forms vary by facts, case stage, and local court. Fresh Start IL prepares supported form drafts for your review; we do not promise that every document or local requirement is covered.

## Step 5: File with the Circuit Court

Confirm venue, filing method, and the current fee directly with the appropriate circuit clerk before filing.

## Step 6: Serve Your Spouse

Service requirements and permitted alternatives depend on the case and court. Follow the current official instructions for your filing.

## Step 7: Wait for the Response

Response deadlines and waiver procedures depend on service and the applicable rules. Verify the deadline shown in the official papers and court instructions.

## Step 8: Final Hearing

Final-hearing procedures vary by judge, county, and case. Only the court can decide whether the papers are sufficient and enter a judgment.

## How Long Does It Take?

There is no reliable universal completion estimate. Court scheduling, service, agreement, required documents, and case-specific issues affect timing.
