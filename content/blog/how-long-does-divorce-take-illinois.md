There is no universal Illinois divorce timeline. The time from filing to judgment depends on the case, service, required disclosures and documents, unresolved issues, and the court's schedule.

## Why Timing Varies

An agreed case may involve fewer disputed steps than a contested case, but “uncontested” does not guarantee a particular hearing date or completion time. A case can take longer when:

- service or notice is delayed,
- the spouses have unresolved property, support, or parenting issues,
- documents must be corrected or supplemented,
- discovery, mediation, evaluations, or a trial are required, or
- the assigned court's calendar is congested.

## What the Six-Month Language Means

Illinois law uses six months of living separate and apart as a rebuttable presumption that the irreconcilable-differences requirement has been met. That language should not be treated as a universal pre-filing separation or waiting period. Check current law and court instructions for your circumstances or ask a lawyer.

## How to Get a Case-Specific Estimate

Before relying on a date:

1. Confirm the current service and filing requirements.
2. Ask the appropriate circuit clerk about scheduling procedures, while remembering that clerk staff cannot give legal advice.
3. Identify any unresolved financial or parenting issues.
4. Ask a lawyer about legal deadlines or case-specific timing when needed.

No website or document-preparation service controls court scheduling or can promise when a judge will enter a judgment.
